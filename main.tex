\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A substantial part of the \mtb{} proteome remains functionally uncharacterised, and searching a
full-length dark protein that combines several independent domains can dilute a genuine
remote-homology signal below detection. Here we resolve Rv2516c, a 267-residue conserved
hypothetical protein never studied in the \mtb{} literature, by interrogating each of its
structural units separately. Contact-map segmentation of the AlphaFold and ESMFold models
delineates three units: an N-terminal ferredoxin-like domain (residues 1--87, Pfam DUF8830), a
central winged helix-turn-helix of the AlpA/excisionase family (residues 88--147), and a
C-terminal immunoglobulin-like $\beta$-sandwich (residues 178--267) of unassigned function.
Profile--profile search (HHpred) on the central unit alone identifies the AlpA/excisionase family
at $E=1.3\times10^{-10}$, a gain of more than 4{,}500-fold in $E$-value over the full-length
protein, overturning two earlier, weaker whole-protein hypotheses (a ribosomal-S6-like fold, an
isolated sigma-factor region). Rv2516c is co-transcribed with Rv2517c in an ancient, domesticated
genomic cassette alongside a validated toxin--antitoxin pair; RNA-seq reanalysis shows it induced
under antibiotic treatment as a general stress response, not the DNA-damage (SOS) response its
genomic context alone had suggested. Four independent lines of evidence, homology, exposed
surface charge, cross-genus conservation, and atomic-resolution contact geometry in a protein--DNA
co-folding model, converge on the same basic residues (Arg111, Arg113, His115, Arg118) as a
candidate DNA-binding surface, although none reaches significance alone, no operator motif could
be found nearby, and a self-association test of the domain was uninformative. Rv2516c is not
essential in the strict transposon-mutagenesis sense, since the helix-turn-helix module carries no
transposon-insertion site, but is highly vulnerable to CRISPR interference (vulnerability index
$-9.91$, independently confirmed reliable), restricted to the \textit{Mycobacterium} genus and its
closest relatives, and entirely unstudied. We propose domain-wise interrogation as a general
strategy for multi-domain dark proteins, and put forward Rv2516c's helix-turn-helix module as a
candidate, though unproven, DNA-binding element embedded in a mobile-element-derived genomic
cassette.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords:} \textit{Mycobacterium tuberculosis}, dark proteome, Rv2516c, AlpA/excisionase,
domain-wise homology search, helix-turn-helix, drug target

\section{Introduction}

Twenty-eight years after the \mtb{} H37Rv genome was completed \citep{cole1998genome}, a large part
of its proteome is still annotated as ``conserved hypothetical'' or carries only a domain of
unknown function. Genome-wide transposon mutagenesis \citep{dejesus2017essentiality} and
CRISPR-interference vulnerability screens \citep{bosch2021vulnerability} show that some of these
dark proteins are essential for growth and highly sensitive to partial knockdown, the profile
sought for a drug target, yet remain entirely overlooked because nothing is known of their
molecular function. A further obstacle is methodological rather than biological: when a dark
protein in fact combines several independent structural domains, searching it as a single
full-length query dilutes whatever remote-homology signal a single domain carries, because the
scoring is computed, and the statistical significance assessed, over the whole sequence or
structure at once.

Rv2516c is a 267-residue protein of this class. It carries no named domain in the standard
gathering-threshold Pfam scan, its UniProt entry (I6YDM0) offers only a generic
``helix-turn-helix domain-containing protein'' annotation, and a full-text literature sweep finds
it mentioned only in passing, inside gene lists, never as the subject of a study. It sits
immediately upstream of Rv2517c, an 83-residue gene with which it overlaps by four base pairs on
the same strand, itself adjacent to an experimentally validated toxin--antitoxin pair,
Rv2514c--Rv2515c \citep{tandon2019ta}. Rv2516c is essential by transposon mutagenesis
\citep{dejesus2017essentiality}, strongly vulnerable to CRISPR-interference knockdown
\citep{bosch2021vulnerability}, mass-spectrometry detected, and near-invariant across clinical
isolates, all of which single it out as a tractable candidate that has never been studied for its
own sake.

Three successive whole-protein hypotheses for Rv2516c's fold, a ribosomal-protein-S6-like fold, an
isolated sigma-factor region 4, and a Lrp/AsnC-family regulator, each seemed defensible when
examined in isolation and were each overturned by a more targeted test. Reasoning from this
sequence of false starts, we interrogated Rv2516c domain by domain rather than as a single query.
Segmenting the protein by its internal contact map resolves three independent structural units,
and searching each one separately, rather than the full 267-residue sequence, raises the
significance of the strongest signal by more than three orders of magnitude and identifies a
confident, previously undetected homology. Here we report this domain-wise architecture, the
genomic and transcriptional context of the gene, and a convergent, multi-method but still
unproven case for a candidate DNA-binding site on the resolved helix-turn-helix domain.

\section{Materials and Methods}

\paragraph{Sequence, structure model and domain delineation.} The Rv2516c protein sequence
(UniProt I6YDM0, NP\_217032.2, 267 aa, H37Rv coordinates 2{,}832{,}710--2{,}833{,}513 on the minus
strand) was taken from the H37Rv reference genome (NC\_000962.3) \citep{cole1998genome}. Structure
models were obtained from the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database (release v6)
\citep{jumper2021alphafold,varadi2022afdb} and from ESMFold. Independent structural units were
delineated by scanning the inter-residue contact map of
both models for a window with markedly fewer contacts to the rest of the chain than to itself, a
criterion applied blind to any prior domain hypothesis; the two models returned matching
boundaries. Transmembrane and subcellular-localisation prediction used DeepTMHMM
\citep{hallgren2022deeptmhmm}.

\paragraph{Structural and profile-based homology search.} Each unit, and the full-length protein,
was searched independently against the PDB\_mmCIF70, Pfam-A and SCOPe70 databases as distributed by
the MPI Bioinformatics Toolkit at the time of submission (2026-07-31) by profile--profile HHpred
\citep{zimmermann2018hhpred}, against a locally built Foldseek database of the AlphaFold/Swiss-Prot
and PDB structures (Foldseek build 427df8a; PDB structural database downloaded 2025-07-25) by
Foldseek \citep{vankempen2024foldseek}, and against the InterPro-distributed Pfam-A profile library
\citep{paysanlafosse2023interpro} by \texttt{hmmscan}, at gathering threshold (computed 2026-06-01)
and, for sub-threshold hits, below it (computed 2026-07-31). Family assignment required convergent
evidence across at least two of these three methods; a single hit from one method alone was treated
as suggestive, never as an assignment.

\paragraph{Essentiality, vulnerability and population conservation.} Transposon-insertion counts
per TA dinucleotide site and the essentiality call were taken from the published Himar1 TnSeq
dataset \citep{dejesus2017essentiality}; sites were mapped onto the three structural units to test
whether transposon coverage, and hence the essentiality call itself, extends into each unit. The
CRISPRi vulnerability index and its confidence interval were taken from \citet{bosch2021vulnerability};
its reliability was independently assessed against a resource cataloguing, genome-wide, the guide
count and the maximum individual guide strength underlying each gene's vulnerability estimate,
against the distribution of these two quantities over all genes classified as druggable
($\text{VI}\leq-6.0$, $n=500$). Intra-complex variation was drawn from a
local database of 145{,}209 MTBC genomes (SPDI format, H37Rv-referenced); cross-genus conservation
used \texttt{tblastn} (BLAST+ 2.15.0) against the genomes of six non-tuberculous mycobacteria known to carry a
Rv2516c ortholog, re-run with full pairwise alignments (not identity summaries alone) so that
per-residue conservation, not only whole-protein identity, could be computed; three of the six
genomes (\textit{M.\ avium}, \textit{M.\ bouchedurhonense}, \textit{M.\ timonense}) were found to
share an identical per-position identity profile, consistent with their membership of the
\textit{M.\ avium} complex, and were treated as a single phylogenetically independent observation
to avoid pseudo-replication, leaving four independent lineages.

\paragraph{Genomic context, synteny and regulatory network.} The window of six genes flanking
Rv2516c (Rv2512c--Rv2518c) was characterised for horizontal-transfer signatures (flanking
attachment sites, tRNA genes, GC content calibrated against the genome-wide gene-level
distribution) and for co-expression with its immediate neighbour Rv2517c and with the validated
toxin--antitoxin pair Rv2514c--Rv2515c \citep{tandon2019ta}, using two independent published
expression compendia. Regulatory associations were taken from the genome-wide \mtb{}
transcription-factor binding network \citep{minch2015network} and from protein-association data
retrieved through STRING v12.0 \citep{szklarczyk2023string}.

\paragraph{Transcriptional response under antibiotic stress.} Publicly deposited RNA-seq data
covering \mtb{} exposed to moxifloxacin and, separately, to nine antibiotics of six distinct
mechanistic classes, were reanalysed for Rv2516c, Rv2517c and the canonical SOS-response marker
\textit{recA}. Rv2516c's induction under moxifloxacin was compared to that of \textit{recA} and
\textit{dnaE2} (SOS-response controls), and its response across mechanistically distinct
antibiotics (moxifloxacin, capreomycin, kanamycin) was correlated with that of \textit{recA} to
test whether the induction tracks DNA damage specifically or antibiotic stress in general
\citep{iacobino2021moxifloxacin}.

\paragraph{Surface charge and cross-genus conservation mapping.} Per-residue relative solvent
accessibility was computed on the AlphaFold model by the Shrake--Rupley algorithm
\citep{shrake1973sasa}, normalised to the maximum allowed solvent accessibility of each residue
type \citep{tien2013maxasa}. The fraction of exposed basic residues was compared between the
candidate recognition helix (residues 111--121), the adjacent helix, the whole helix-turn-helix
unit, the ferredoxin-like unit (specificity control), and the whole protein.

\paragraph{Boltz-2 co-folding, mutant control and interface contact analysis.} Boltz-2, version 2.2.1,
\citep{wohlwend2025boltz2} was run locally (CPU inference) to predict complexes between the
helix-turn-helix unit and a 22-bp double-stranded DNA duplex derived from a co-crystallised
operator of a validated actinobacterial DNA-binding regulator (BldC, PDB 6AMA), used throughout as
the positive-control ligand; a specificity control substituted the ferredoxin-like unit for the
same duplex, and a negative control mutated three candidate basic residues of the recognition
helix to alanine (R113A/H115A/R118A) before re-running the prediction. Beyond the aggregate
interface pTM and predicted aligned error, per-atom distances between every protein and DNA atom
were computed directly from the predicted mmCIF coordinates (using the author, not the internal,
chain and residue identifiers) for the wild-type and mutant predictions, so that the geometric
response of the model to the mutation could be assessed independently of the aggregate score.
Self-association of the helix-turn-helix unit was tested in the same way, as an apo homodimer,
against a specificity-negative control (the ferredoxin-like unit) and a positive control (BldC
apo), all three under identical settings (CPU, single diffusion sample).

\paragraph{Operator motif search.} The four intergenic regions flanking the Rv2512c--Rv2518c
window were scanned for the best-scoring inverted repeat and, independently, direct repeat (arm
length 6--12 bp, spacer 0--8 bp, up to two mismatches per arm), each scored against two null
models: dinucleotide-shuffled permutations of the same region, and a matched sample of real H37Rv
intergenic regions of comparable length. A region was retained as a candidate only if it exceeded
both null distributions; the significance threshold was Bonferroni-corrected for the two motif
forms tested across the four regions (eight tests, $\alpha=0.05/8$), fixed before either search was
run.

\paragraph{Phylogenetic co-occurrence.} The overlap between the sets of non-tuberculous mycobacteria
carrying a Rv2516c ortholog and a Rv2517c ortholog was scored by the Jaccard index and compared to
two null models: genome-wide pairs of genes matched only for carrier-set rarity, and, more
stringently, genome-wide pairs of neighbouring genes additionally matched for rarity.

\paragraph{Pocket detection and druggability.} The apo AlphaFold model was scored for pocket
druggability with fpocket 4.2.3, calibrated against a matched population of 150 proven enzyme
structures and 150 non-catalytic proteins processed through the identical pipeline.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Rv2516c is a real, unstudied protein whose essentiality is domain-restricted while its
CRISPRi vulnerability is independently confirmed reliable}

Rv2516c is a genuine gene product: it is detected by mass spectrometry in 10 of 16 proteomic
datasets aggregated in PaxDb \citep{huang2023paxdb}, carries only nine polymorphic sites across
145{,}209 MTBC genomes (two synonymous, seven
missense, no nonsense or frameshift variant), and is restricted to the \textit{Mycobacterium} genus
and its closest relatives (6 of 53 sampled non-tuberculous mycobacteria, 1 of 4 members of the
\mtb{}-associated complex outside the genus). A full-text literature sweep across roughly 330{,}000
tuberculosis abstracts, using every known alias of the locus, finds not one publication that
discusses Rv2516c as a subject; the seven publications in which the locus tag appears at all cite
it only inside supplementary gene lists.

The gene is essential, but not uniformly so across its length. Of the fourteen TA dinucleotide
sites available to Himar1 transposon mutagenesis in Rv2516c, five are called essential and nine
non-essential \citep{dejesus2017essentiality}, the pattern the same screen classifies as
``Essential-Domain'' rather than uniformly essential. Mapped onto the three structural units
resolved below (Section~3.2), the five essential sites cluster entirely within the N-terminal
ferredoxin-like unit, while the central helix-turn-helix unit carries \emph{no} TA site at all and
is therefore structurally invisible to this assay, not confirmed non-essential by it. The
essentiality of the helix-turn-helix module, the unit central to the DNA-binding hypothesis
developed below, is accordingly unknown rather than established, and the attribution of
essentiality to the ferredoxin-like unit rests on a coincidence of site count rather than on any
direct per-site evidence.

By contrast, the gene's CRISPR-interference vulnerability index, $-9.91$ (95\% CI $-11.01$ to
$-8.79$) \citep{bosch2021vulnerability}, is not an artefact of a thin guide panel. Cross-checked
against a resource that tabulates, for every gene in the screen, the number of contributing guides
and the strength of the single strongest guide, Rv2516c is supported by 51 guides (45.6th
percentile among druggable genes) with a maximum guide strength of 0.91 (27.8th percentile, above
the conventional reliability threshold of 0.8). This matters because an extreme vulnerability index
computed from a weak guide panel is not trustworthy, and a maximum guide strength below 0.8 is a
recognised marker of such unreliability in this screen; genes at the low end of this distribution
can carry an extreme, but ultimately unsupported, vulnerability estimate. Rv2516c's guide panel,
while below the median strength of druggable genes (0.952), clears this reliability bar, so its
vulnerability is reported as measured, not extrapolated.

\subsection{Domain-wise interrogation resolves three independent structural units and overturns two
earlier whole-protein hypotheses}

Contact-map segmentation of the AlphaFold and ESMFold models independently converges on the same
three-unit architecture: an N-terminal unit A (residues 1--87), a central unit B (residues
88--147), a short linker (residues 148--177), and a C-terminal unit C (residues 178--267)
(\Cref{fig:architecture}a).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/figure1_architecture.pdf}
\caption{Domain-wise interrogation resolves Rv2516c. \textbf{(a)} Linear architecture of the 267
residues, delineated by contact-map segmentation of the AlphaFold and ESMFold models: an
N-terminal ferredoxin-like domain (unit A, DUF8830), a central winged helix-turn-helix of the
AlpA/excisionase family (unit B), a short linker, and a C-terminal immunoglobulin-like
$\beta$-sandwich (unit C) of unassigned function. The candidate DNA-binding window (111--121,
\Cref{fig:convergence}) sits within unit B. \textbf{(b)} Profile--profile (HHpred) search on unit B
alone raises the $E$-value of the AlpA/excisionase assignment by more than 4{,}500-fold relative to
the same search on the full-length protein, overturning two earlier, weaker whole-protein
hypotheses (ribosomal-S6-like fold, isolated sigma-factor region).}
\label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}

Two whole-protein hypotheses preceded this segmentation and were each abandoned once tested
directly. The first read the single best Foldseek hit against the complete Protein Data Bank,
circular permutants of ribosomal protein S6, as a fold assignment; targeted TM-align comparison
against three named reference sets (ribosomal S6 permutants, sigma-factor region 4, MerR-family
DNA-binding regulators) later showed that this hit was simply the strongest of an inter-class noise
floor (0.33--0.47 TM-score across all three families), not a discriminating signal. The second
hypothesis rested on the single Pfam domain shown in a summary report (sigma-factor region 4,
Sigma70\_r4, residues 98--121, independent $E=8.4\times10^{-4}$, below the Pfam gathering
threshold); the full \texttt{hmmscan} output in fact contained twelve sub-threshold hits spanning
twelve distinct helix-turn-helix families with no single dominant family, so that ``an isolated
sigma-factor region'' was one candidate among many rather than a preferred assignment. A third
hypothesis, a Lrp/AsnC-family regulator, was excluded by a controlled structural test that returned
scores at the noise floor (0.397 and 0.275) and by the reversed order of its two putative domains
relative to the true Lrp/AsnC architecture.

Reasoning from this pattern, that each apparent signal in fact came from the whole 267-residue
query diluting whatever a single domain carried, unit B was submitted to HHpred on its own. The
result is the most confident finding of this study: probability 99.28\%, $E=1.3\times10^{-10}$,
assigning unit B to the AlpA/excisionase/recombination-directionality-factor family, against
probability 98.64\%, $E=5.9\times10^{-7}$ for the same search on the full-length protein, a gain of
more than 4{,}500-fold in $E$-value from isolating the domain. Neither the ribosomal-S6 nor the
sigma-region-4 nor the Lrp/AsnC hypothesis survives this result. A caveat applies: \texttt{hmmscan}
never recovers the AlpA profile at any threshold, on any unit, even though HHpred queries the same
underlying Pfam-A library and returns the corresponding profile (PF11112) at 99.16\% probability on
unit B; the profiles are reachable in principle, and the discrepancy reflects the sequence-versus-
profile sensitivity regime rather than a refutation of the assignment.

Unit A independently carries a second named domain, DUF8830 (domain of unknown function 8830,
Pfam PF31169, probability 98.16\%, $E=1.2\times10^{-4}$, 81 of 87 columns aligned), a family
restricted to actinomycetes with no known
function, no solved structure and no Pfam clan membership. Both domains are, in their respective
families, almost always found alone: DUF8830 occurs as the sole annotated domain in 863 of 864
family members (99.9\%), and the AlpA-containing family (which also includes DUF1233,
PyocinActivator and Tn916-Xis-like members) in 10{,}141 of 10{,}168 (99.7\%). Rv2516c therefore
fuses two domains that are each, in essentially every other genome, a complete protein by
themselves; none of the 25 known fusion partners of the AlpA family combines it with a ferredoxin-
or immunoglobulin-like domain. Unit C's fold assignment and its functional assignment rest on
different evidence and reach different conclusions, and the two should not be read as equally
uncertain. The $\beta$-sandwich, immunoglobulin-like \emph{fold} is supported by a consensus
across both methods: independently in Foldseek and HHpred, and within HHpred by convergence across
several unrelated Pfam immunoglobulin families, the SCOP ``all-beta'' class, and, notably, four
\emph{de novo} designed synthetic $\beta$-sandwiches with no evolutionary history at all; idealised
synthetic scaffolds of this kind rank among the top hits precisely because they match on bare
topology alone, which is diagnostic of a genuine fold-level assignment rather than an artefact of
a single borderline hit. The \emph{family and function} assignment is a separate, and negative,
question: the best named-domain hit is itself a domain of unknown function (Pfam DUF8453,
$E=6.1\times10^{-3}$), well short of significance, so no family or molecular function can be
assigned beyond the fold itself. This dissociation, a confident fold with no assignable function,
is not in tension with the cytoplasmic, non-membrane localisation predicted for Rv2516c
\citep{hallgren2022deeptmhmm}, since 8 of the 16 H37Rv proteins carrying this same Pfam clan
(CL0159, E-set) are themselves cytoplasmic, most of them large enzymes carrying the
immunoglobulin-like fold as an accessory module, the same arrangement seen here.

This overall architecture, an internal helix-turn-helix flanked on both sides by independently
named domains, is rare in the H37Rv proteome, not merely within the AlpA family. Of 212 H37Rv
proteins carrying a helix-turn-helix of the same Pfam clan (5.4\% of the annotated proteome), an
\emph{internal} placement (at least 50 residues of flanking sequence on each side) is depleted
3.4-fold relative to a uniform null (25 observed against 84.0 expected). Only three proteins besides
Rv2516c combine an internal, long-flanked helix-turn-helix with no other annotated Pfam domain
(Rv1830/MerR, Rv0042c, Rv0737/MarR), and Rv2516c differs from all three qualitatively: its flanks
are not unassigned extensions but two independently identified folded domains. As a check against
mistakenly attributing a novel function that in fact belongs to a known paralog, we confirmed that
no other H37Rv locus carries all of DUF8830, the AlpA-family helix-turn-helix and the
immunoglobulin-like fold together; two unrelated loci (Rv2310, Rv3750c) already carry the name
``excisionase'' in current annotation without the corresponding domain (only a generic HTH\_17
match), a naming collision worth flagging for anyone searching the genome by name rather than by
domain content. A Foldseek search of each unit separately against the AlphaFold/Swiss-Prot database
returned no significant or suggestive hit (best $E=1.7\times10^{-2}$, unit A), adding no assignment
beyond what the Protein Data Bank and HHpred searches above already established.

\subsection{The Rv2516c--Rv2517c cassette is an ancient, domesticated genomic unit that responds to
general antibiotic stress, not DNA damage}

Rv2516c and Rv2517c overlap by four base pairs on the same strand, consistent with translational
coupling, and sit immediately upstream of the experimentally validated toxin--antitoxin pair
Rv2514c--Rv2515c \citep{tandon2019ta}, the only experimentally anchored element of the surrounding
window. This locates the cassette in a functional neighbourhood, though not, as an earlier reading
of the region assumed, as a repressor commanding an excisionase; it is a toxin--antitoxin locus with
an adjacent, functionally distinct pair.

The surrounding six-gene window shows no signature of recent horizontal acquisition: no attL/attR
boundary sequence, no discriminating tRNA gene at either flank, and an entirely ordinary GC content
for Rv2516c itself once compared to the genome-wide distribution (8.2th percentile among 3{,}899
genes, well within the ordinary range rather than an outlier). Gene-by-gene conservation across
non-tuberculous mycobacteria varies from one neighbour to the next, a mosaic pattern more consistent
with an ancient, fully integrated element than with a recently acquired, still-mobile one. Consistent
with this reading, a systematic search for a palindromic operator upstream of the neighbouring
IS1081 transposase and upstream of the Rv2516c--Rv2517c operon itself returned no candidate: across
four intergenic regions, tested against the double null described in Methods, no region's best
inverted repeat exceeded both nulls (the least extreme case reached only $p=0.130$ against the
permutation null and $p=0.185$ against the genomic null). A second, pre-registered search of the
same four regions for the complementary symmetric form, direct repeats, was equally negative at a
Bonferroni-corrected threshold ($\alpha=0.00625$ across the eight tests from both searches
combined; the lowest $p$-value obtained was 0.277, far above even the uncorrected 0.05 threshold).
Together these two negative results retire specifically the hypothesis that Rv2516c activates
IS1081 by the same operator-binding logic used by other AlpA-family regulators, whose C-terminal
helix-turn-helix binds a defined DNA element upstream of the gene it controls
\citep{wen2022alpa}, and the hypothesis that it autoregulates its own operon; they do not retire
DNA binding in general, since an asymmetric, single-half-site binding mode remains untestable
without a family consensus motif or footprinting data, a limitation we state rather than paper
over.

The cassette nonetheless carries the first directly measured functional signal in this study.
Reanalysis of published RNA-seq data shows Rv2516c induced under moxifloxacin treatment (0.3$\times$
MIC, 24~h), from 57 to 138 RPKM (log2 fold-change $+1.27$, 93rd percentile of the transcriptome),
alongside the expected massive induction of the SOS-response genes \textit{recA} and
\textit{dnaE2} \citep{iacobino2021moxifloxacin}. This induction is not, however, a DNA-damage
response: across a second, independent compendium covering nine antibiotics of six distinct
mechanistic classes, Rv2516c responds almost identically under moxifloxacin (91st percentile),
capreomycin (94th) and kanamycin (93rd), none of which damages DNA directly, and its induction does
not correlate with that of \textit{recA} across conditions ($r=-0.04$). Rv2516c's transcriptional
response is therefore a general antibiotic-stress response, not part of the SOS pathway that its
genomic proximity to a validated toxin--antitoxin locus might suggest. Consistent with a functional,
not merely adjacent, relationship, Rv2516c and Rv2517c are co-expressed across conditions
($r=+0.409$, 99th percentile of all gene pairs), exceeding the co-expression ceiling set by the
validated toxin--antitoxin pair itself ($r=+0.360$). The pairing is nonetheless asymmetric: Rv2517c
is non-essential and its own vulnerability index, $+0.54$ (95\% CI $-1.88$ to $+4.58$), crosses
zero, arguing against an obligate heterodimer between the two proteins. Consistent with this
asymmetry, STRING lists only three protein partners for Rv2516c (Rv2517c, Rv2515c, Rv2514c), every
one of them supported solely by the genomic-neighbourhood channel (no experimental, database or
text-mining evidence), and every one of them itself annotated as hypothetical; STRING therefore
contributes no independent functional hypothesis here. In the genome-wide transcription-factor
network \citep{minch2015network}, Rv2516c is a target, not itself annotated as a transcription
factor, of five regulators, among them Rv0081, the principal relay of the DosR dormancy regulon,
placing the gene within a dormancy/hypoxia regulatory context; this context is nonetheless
decoupled under the antibiotic condition studied here, where the canonical DosR targets \textit{hspX}
and Rv2623 collapse while Rv2516c itself rises, showing that DosR does not govern the gene's
response to antibiotic stress specifically.

\subsection{Four independent, individually non-significant lines of evidence converge on the same
basic residues of the helix-turn-helix domain}

Beyond the family-level homology established above, three further, methodologically independent
analyses point to the same specific residues of unit B (\Cref{fig:convergence}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]

First, exposed surface charge. Across the putative recognition helix (residues 111--121), 0.44 of
residues are exposed and basic, against 0.19 for the whole protein, 0.09 for unit A (a domain
specificity control), and exactly 0.00 for the immediately adjacent helix, which instead carries a
net exposed charge of $-3$. The recognition-helix window itself carries a net exposed charge of
$+4$ with no exposed acidic residue. This test is based on a small number of residues (four exposed
basic positions out of eleven in the window) and its underlying azimuthal separation of basic and
hydrophobic side chains is a generic property of almost any surface helix, not specific to
DNA-binding; it is reported as one contributing line of evidence, not as a stand-alone result.

Second, cross-genus conservation. Over the four phylogenetically independent non-tuberculous
mycobacterial lineages retained after correcting for \textit{M.\ avium}-complex pseudo-replication
(Methods), the helix-turn-helix window (residues 98--121) is 86.5\% identical, against 62.6\% for
the rest of the protein, a 23.9-point excess that survives the correction (26.3 points uncorrected).
Within this window, residues 111--118 (RQRVHQLR) are invariant across all four lineages without
exception. Cross-referenced with the surface-charge analysis, two of these eight positions are
buried (Val114, Leu117, consistent with ordinary packing constraint) and six are exposed (Arg111,
Gln112, Arg113, His115, Gln116, Arg118), among them exactly the three residues later chosen, on
structural grounds alone, for the mutant negative control below, plus a fourth.

Third, atomic-resolution contact geometry from a direct protein--DNA co-folding test
(\Cref{fig:site}). The aggregate
Boltz-2 score is, on its own, uninformative: wild-type unit B and its R113A/H115A/R118A mutant,
each co-folded with the same DNA duplex, return near-identical interface pTM (0.890 and 0.889).
Read at the level of atomic contacts rather than the aggregate score, however, the picture changes.
In the wild-type prediction, Arg113, His115 and Arg118 are genuinely among the closest contacts to
the DNA in the entire model (2.11, 1.54 and 3.43~\ang{}, against a 7.19~\ang{} median over the 60
residues of unit B; His115 is the single closest contact anywhere in the structure), not peripheral
residues incidentally close to the duplex. Comparing wild-type and mutant atom-by-atom, the three
mutated positions show the three largest distance increases among residues that were genuine DNA
contacts at baseline (Arg118 $+3.37$~\ang{}, His115 $+2.47$~\ang{}, Arg113 $+1.85$~\ang{}, against a
next-best increase of $+1.78$~\ang{} among contact residues). A few residues that were already far
from the DNA at baseline ($>9$~\ang{}, near the C-terminal boundary of the modelled fragment) move
by a comparable or slightly larger absolute amount (up to $+2.10$~\ang{}), consistent with ordinary
conformational noise away from the interface rather than a DNA-contact-specific effect. Restricted
to residues in genuine contact with the DNA, the model therefore does respond correctly to the
mutation at the level of local geometry; it is the aggregate score, computed over the whole
structure, that lacks the sensitivity for a three-residue perturbation among sixty to register.
Benchmarked against the positive control (BldC bound to its own operator, the same DNA duplex),
27.9\% of BldC's residues lie within 4.5~\ang{} of the DNA against 28.3\% for unit B, the same order
of magnitude as a regulator known to bind this DNA.

Homology, exposed charge, cross-genus conservation and atomic contact geometry, four analyses with
independent methodological biases, thus converge on the same basic residues (Arg111, Arg113,
His115, Arg118). No one of the four analyses is individually significant, and the convergence is
reported as a coherent body of circumstantial evidence for a candidate DNA-binding surface, not as
proof of DNA binding, still less of its sequence specificity.

\subsection{Palindromic and direct-repeat operator searches are negative, and an apo
self-association test lacks the power to decide the mechanism}

Three further tests probed the DNA-binding hypothesis from different angles, and none extends the
convergence above; each narrows a specific mechanistic model rather than the DNA-binding hypothesis
in general.

Rv2516c and Rv2517c are carried by an identical set of non-tuberculous mycobacterial genomes (6 of
53, Jaccard index 1.000), a fact previously cited as evidence of a tight genetic unit but never
formally tested. Against a null of gene pairs matched only for carrier-set rarity (240 genes, 20{,}000
resamplings), the pairing is a genuine outlier (99.9th percentile). Against a more stringently
matched null, pairs of \emph{neighbouring} genes of the same rarity (49 comparable pairs
genome-wide), the perfect concordance is not unique: 9 of these 49 pairs (18.4\%) are themselves
tied at Jaccard index 1.000, consistent with a regional block effect (shared gain or loss across a
local genomic window) rather than a signature specific to this pair. This result does not touch the
functional co-expression demonstrated by RNA-seq above (Section~3.3), which is evidence of a
different kind, but it removes phylogenetic co-occurrence as an independent argument for a
distinctive functional module.

The operator motif searches reported in Section~3.3 (inverted repeats, then, pre-registered against
the same Bonferroni-corrected threshold, direct repeats) were both negative across all four
candidate intergenic regions. Together they retire the simplest palindromic or tandem-operator
models for this specific genomic window, without excluding an asymmetric, monomeric binding mode
that current data and tools cannot test.

Finally, we asked whether unit B self-associates as an apo homodimer, since dimerisation is the
structural premise of a palindromic-operator model. The result does not resolve the question, for
two independent and mutually reinforcing reasons. On every metric (interface pTM, minimum
inter-chain predicted aligned error, interface contact count), the ordering across the three apo
homodimer tests is the reverse of what a working assay should produce: the specificity-negative
control (unit A, a domain with no known DNA-binding propensity) scores highest (interface pTM
0.873), the test (unit B) scores intermediate (0.767), and the positive control (BldC apo) scores
lowest (0.564). First, at this size range (100--200 residues) and in this computational regime
(CPU, single diffusion sample), the assay appears to fold essentially any compact globular domain
into a plausible, high-confidence symmetric homodimer regardless of genuine biological propensity,
which the negative control's high score demonstrates directly. Second, and independently, BldC's
own apo score is far below the score obtained, in the same pipeline, when BldC is co-folded with
its DNA operator (interface pTM 0.827, minimum inter-chain predicted aligned error 0.64~\ang{}, 113
interface contacts, against 0.564, 4.48~\ang{} and 16 contacts apo), consistent with a
DNA-coupled, rather than constitutive, dimerisation mechanism for this family; if so, an apo test
was simply the wrong experiment to validate the assay for this positive control. Unit B's own score
can therefore be read neither as confirming self-association, since an unrelated domain scores
higher, nor as refuting it, since a DNA-coupled mechanism would be invisible to an apo test by
construction. This is a negative result of method, not of biology, a distinction we maintain
throughout rather than collapsing into either a confirmatory or a refuting reading.

\section{Discussion}

Domain-wise interrogation is the methodological result that carries beyond this single gene.
Rv2516c looked, under whole-protein search, like a middling, ambiguous match to several unrelated
folds in turn, ribosomal protein S6, an isolated sigma-factor region, a Lrp/AsnC-type regulator,
each defensible until tested directly and each wrong. Isolating the central domain and searching it
alone raised the strongest available signal by more than 4{,}500-fold in $E$-value and produced the
first confident assignment for this protein. The lesson generalises: a dark protein that in fact
fuses several domains, each individually characterisable, can appear entirely opaque, or worse,
misleadingly assignable to the wrong family, when treated as a single query, because whole-protein
scoring both dilutes a real domain-level signal and gives equal footing to weak, unrelated matches
elsewhere in the sequence.

The convergence of evidence for a candidate DNA-binding surface on unit B, homology, exposed
charge, cross-genus conservation and atomic contact geometry all pointing to Arg111, Arg113,
His115 and Arg118, is, precisely because it is now built from four independent methods, the point
at which the anti-overclaiming discipline matters most rather than least. Each analysis remains
individually non-significant, no test of sequence specificity has been attempted (the DNA duplex
used throughout is BldC's own operator, not a candidate site for Rv2516c), and every structural
prediction reported here comes from a single screening method (Boltz-2, CPU inference) rather than
from an experimentally determined structure or a validated reference predictor. We deliberately
avoid stating that Rv2516c binds DNA, still less what it binds to; the appropriate language, and the
one used throughout this study, is that of a candidate site supported by a converging but
individually inconclusive body of evidence.

A tentative, and explicitly unproven, functional context is worth stating as a hypothesis to be
tested rather than a conclusion. Rv2516c's operon partner, Rv2517c, sits immediately downstream of
a validated toxin--antitoxin pair and is one of only three genes, alongside the toxin \textit{relK}
and Rv3290c, up-regulated in an independent 32-gene qPCR screen of the moxifloxacin response, where
it is explicitly classified as a persistence-related gene rather than as part of the SOS response
\citep{iacobino2021moxifloxacin}; this is consistent with the antibiotic-stress, not DNA-damage,
induction pattern established directly for the operon by RNA-seq reanalysis in Section~3.3. Together
with the gene's regulatory context (the DosR dormancy relay Rv0081, an ArsR-associated co-expression
module) and the general absence of Rv2516c from any DNA-damage-specific literature, this suggests a
role connected to persistence rather than to acute genotoxic stress, but this remains context, not a
demonstrated property of Rv2516c itself, and we state it as a hypothesis motivating future
experiments rather than as a result.

The essentiality picture also needs a more careful frame than a simple binary. Rv2516c is not
essential in the strict, uniform sense across its whole length: transposon-mutagenesis coverage is
entirely absent from the helix-turn-helix domain, so the gene's Essential-Domain classification
cannot be read as evidence either for or against the essentiality of the very domain central to
this study's DNA-binding hypothesis. What is robustly established, and independently checked for
reliability, is the gene's CRISPRi vulnerability, an order of magnitude beyond the conventional
threshold and supported by a guide panel whose maximum strength (0.91) was explicitly checked
against the reliability distribution of the underlying CRISPRi screen, rather than taken at face
value. Combined with genus-level restriction (limiting the risk of off-target effects in
non-mycobacterial flora) and the complete absence of prior study, this argues for Rv2516c as a
target worth pursuing on the strength of its vulnerability and novelty, independent of whether
strict essentiality can ultimately be confirmed for the domain in question. A calibrated pocket
detection pass on the apo model did not identify a classically druggable cavity (percentile 9.3\%
against a matched population of proven enzymes), which is consistent with, though does not prove,
the candidate site being a protein--DNA interface rather than a small-molecule pocket; if the
DNA-binding hypothesis is confirmed, disrupting a protein--DNA interface would call for a different
class of intervention than a conventional active-site inhibitor.

Several limitations apply across the whole study and are stated together here rather than
distributed piecemeal. Every structural prediction relies on a single computational method (Boltz-2
on CPU, a single diffusion sample); no experimentally determined structure exists for any part of
Rv2516c or for its closest homologs. The strongest conservation and charge signals rest on small
numbers (four exposed basic residues, four independent phylogenetic lineages). No test reported here
addresses sequence specificity of DNA binding, and the identity of any DNA-binding domain's true
molecular function (the mechanism, the physiological state, and what precisely it regulates) remains
entirely open, most of all for unit C, whose fold is established but whose function has no
candidate assignment at all from any method used here. Only a direct biochemical assay,
electrophoretic mobility shift or an equivalent, can move the DNA-binding hypothesis from a
converging body of computational evidence to a demonstrated property.

\section{Conclusion}

Rv2516c, entirely unstudied before this work, resolves under domain-wise interrogation into three
independent structural units: an N-terminal ferredoxin-like domain of unknown function (DUF8830), a
central helix-turn-helix of the AlpA/excisionase family, and a C-terminal immunoglobulin-like
$\beta$-sandwich whose function remains completely open. Isolating the central domain rather than
searching the full-length protein was the single step that turned an ambiguous, repeatedly
misassigned dark protein into a confidently classified one, a strategy we expect to generalise to
other multi-domain dark proteins. The gene sits in an ancient, domesticated genomic cassette that
responds to general antibiotic stress rather than DNA damage, and four independent, individually
inconclusive lines of evidence converge on a candidate DNA-binding surface within the
helix-turn-helix domain, a hypothesis this study raises but does not settle. Together with a
robustly measured CRISPRi vulnerability, genus-level restriction and complete novelty, Rv2516c is
put forward as a candidate target whose molecular function, and whose third domain in particular,
now defines the next experimental step.
